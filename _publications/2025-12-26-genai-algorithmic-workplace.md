## Abstract  
This study investigates how **generative artificial intelligence (GenAI)** is transforming the architecture of the workplace and reconfiguring managerial agency in contemporary organisations. While prior research has explored task automation and human–machine collaboration, scholarship has under-examined the broader structural and epistemic implications of GenAI on authority, coordination, and organisational decision-making.

## Methodology  
* **Data corpus:** 212 Scopus-indexed publications (2018–2025).  
* **Tools:** VOSviewer and Bibliometrix for bibliometric mapping.  
* **Analysis:** Performance trends, thematic structures, and conceptual evolution of the field.

## Key Findings  
- The findings reveal a **dynamic knowledge domain** where technical constructs such as **large language models** and **generative adversarial networks** intersect with behavioural and managerial concepts.
- Mapping of **performance trends** across the literature shows accelerating interest since 2022.
- **Thematic clusters** emerged linking AI capabilities to organisational restructuring, decision-making paradigms, and work design frameworks.
- Unique contribution: bridges the gap between **technical AI research** and **organisational management studies**.

## Practical Implications  
1. **Strategic framework** for organisations adopting GenAI in decision-making processes.  
2. **Guidelines for managers** navigating the shift from traditional to algorithmic authority structures.  
3. **Research agenda** identifying critical gaps for future scholarly investigation.  
4. Understanding the intersection of **human expertise** and **machine intelligence** in modern work design.
